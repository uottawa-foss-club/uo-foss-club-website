Free and Open Source at uOttawa

We turn students into architects of open-source systems.

The uOttawa FOSS Club is a student-led engineering collective dedicated to mastering FOSS, systems architecture, and security-first development. We bridge academic theory with industry-grade engineering by contributing to the global software ecosystem.

We move students beyond being users of technology to becoming builders of it. From landing first PRs to analyzing real-world vulnerabilities, we learn by doing and ship work that lasts.

Core pillars

The foundation of our culture.

Active Contribution

We provide a structured environment for students to contribute to major projects like Next.js. We help members navigate Good First Issues and land their first merged PRs.

Systems-Level Mastery

Our flagship annual event, Linux From Scratch Week, involves building an entire operating system from source. Students demystify the kernel, toolchains, and hardware-software interfaces.

Security-First Engineering

We analyze real-world vulnerabilities and bug bounty reports to understand how software is exploited. Members learn to build hardened, resilient software.

Year plan

Hands-on experiences all year long.

First PR Workshops

Learn how to make your first PR, navigate maintainer feedback, and practice the etiquette of working in global FOSS communities.

Secure Software Workshops

Hands-on sessions on building software that is hard to exploit, with practical lessons on sandboxing and secure defaults.

Linux From Scratch

An annual deep-dive week where we build a Linux system from source and learn how every layer of the stack fits together.

Weekly Co-Hacking

A consistent shared space to collaborate, review code, and push contributions forward together.

Security Review Circles

We review weekly CVEs, dig into post-mortems, and discuss ethical disclosure in our active Discord.

Merged Gala

End-of-semester celebration recognizing students whose open-source contributions have been merged.

Focus tracks

Pick a depth area and go further.

Web Architecture

Learn how global products are designed, deployed, and scaled. Study modern frameworks and contribute to widely used web tooling.

Low-Level Systems Engineering

Dive into kernels, compilers, and hardware interfaces. Build a sharper understanding of how computers actually work.

Security Auditing

Explore real exploit chains, bug bounty reports, and supply chain attacks. Learn to engineer defenses from day one.

No gatekeeping

Build software that lasts and secure the open web.

Whether you are a first-year student or a seasoned developer, you will find a community ready to help you contribute.
